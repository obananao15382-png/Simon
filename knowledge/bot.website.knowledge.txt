# Privacy Policy - Litterbox
Privacy Policy - Litterbox

Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 1, 2026

is a small, non-commercial, fan-made site for Cat Goes Fishing mods, run by one person in their
spare time - not a company. This page explains, in plain language, what information the site collects,
why, and what say you have over it. If anything here is unclear, just email
[email protected] .

1. Who runs Litterbox

Litterbox ( litterbox.fyi ) is
operated independently, as a hobby, by an individual based in Poland. It isn't a registered company, and
it doesn't run ads or make money from the site in any way - it exists purely to help Cat Goes Fishing
modders share their work in one place. For anything related to privacy or your data, contact
[email protected] .

2. What we collect

Account info - if you sign up with email and password, we store your email address and your password (encrypted, we never see it in plain text) through our database provider, Supabase.

Discord sign-in - if you choose "Continue with Discord" instead, Discord shares your account email and basic profile info with us through Discord's own login system (OAuth), and you don't set a separate password.

Profile info - the username you pick, plus anything optional you add: a short bio and an avatar image.

Content you upload - mod files, names, descriptions, icons, banners, and any changelog or announcement posts you write.

Activity on the site - which mods you favorite, follow, or rate, and any reports you submit about a mod.

Basic technical data - standard web server logs from GitHub Pages (which hosts these pages) and Supabase (our database and file host), such as IP address, browser type, and request timestamps. This is routine infrastructure logging, not something we've set up ourselves to track or analyze you.

3. Why we use it

To run your account and the site's features - signing up, logging in, uploading mods, favoriting, following, notifications.

To keep the community safe - reviewing new mods before they go public, handling reports, and preventing abuse.

Where you've chosen it - for example, signing in through Discord instead of a password.

4. What's public vs. private

Heads up: your username, avatar, bio, badges, and the mods you've uploaded and
favorited are all shown publicly on your profile page, visible to anyone - logged in or not.
Your email address, password, and any reports you submit are never shown publicly.

Keep this in mind when choosing a username or avatar, and when favoriting mods if you'd rather that stay private.

5. Who we share data with

Supabase ( supabase.com ) - hosts the site's database, file storage, and login system. Depending on how the project is configured, this may store data outside Poland or the EEA.

Discord - only if you choose to sign in with Discord; Discord Inc. handles that login step directly.

GitHub Pages - hosts the static pages you're browsing right now.

We don't sell your data, run advertising, or share it with marketers. We don't currently use any analytics or tracking tools.

6. Cookies & local storage

Litterbox doesn't use tracking or advertising cookies. To keep you logged in, your browser stores your
session using local storage - a technology similar to cookies. This is strictly necessary for the site
to function and doesn't require separate consent under EU rules. If that ever changes (for example, if
we add analytics in the future), we'll update this page first.

7. How long we keep things

We keep your account and content for as long as your account exists. You can delete any mod you've
uploaded yourself, any time, from its Manage page. To delete your whole account, email us and we'll take
care of it. We may keep minimal records of serious rule violations (like a removed mod containing
malware) for abuse-prevention purposes even after an account is deleted.

8. Your rights

Under GDPR, you can ask us to:

Give you a copy of the data we hold about you (access)

Correct inaccurate data (rectification)

# Cave Shark
"The Cave Shark spends most of its existence in silent reflection, subsisting only on the occasional stray Bombat."

Statistics [ ]

Size

Sell Price

Location

Sea Caverns

The Cave Shark is a Huge grey fish with a streamlined body and eyes that appear half-closed. It is considered one of the hardest fish in the game to catch, and is also one of the most valuable (beaten by the extremely high-value fish like the Dragon , Josie and Sodafish ).

Location [ ]

The Cave in which the cave shark is found on the map of the Sea Caverns.

The Cave Shark is found in a small cavern to the west of the central Caverns . It remains motionless, making it reasonably easy to find, but even getting it to bite is not easy.

Catching Guide [ ]

DISCLAMER: DONT USE HUGE OR MEGA HOOK

The first challenge in catching the Cave Shark is convincing it to bite. As its Catalog entry suggests, it requires a Bombat to be used as bait (the Bombat does not need to be alive).

You will need:

A rock (Bombats will also bite a bomb, but since this will explode and cause them to flee, this does not allow them to be used as bait for another fish) and the Scouting rod upgrade (otherwise the line will be reeled in upon catching a rock)

Sinker upgrade

Lightbulb (Optional but recommended)

Gauge and Improved Crank upgrades (at least three of each) if in Realism mode .

Bomb Stack and Detonator upgrades are highly advised if in Realism mode.

Rocks can be found across the seafloor above where the Cave Shark is found. Bombats are found throughout the central Caverns, including in the area around the Cave Shark, which makes attracting one easy as long as you have a rock on your line beforehand. After this, you must lead your line into the Cave Shark's lair and attract its attention.

Using Bombs instead of Rocks and the Detonator upgrade makes the fight much easier as detonating a bomb while the Cave Shark is on the line will stun it and allow you to reel it for awhile. Usually a Bombat will set off the bomb if it bites however if the bombat is eaten before that happens it won't activate the bomb. But you can catch that fish without grab bombs in realism mode, but thats very hard.

Once hooked, the Cave Shark is an extremely challenging catch in Realism mode that requires a lot of patience. It will lead itself out of the Caverns, and the player must stop it catching itself on the floor. Once out of the Caverns it will repeatedly attempt to snag the line on the same section of seafloor. Reel aggressively, and use the boat while reeling to pull the Cave Shark away from the seafloor where it is trying to snag the line. It will (eventually) tire.

Bugs [ ]

The Cave Shark tends to behave very oddly if you try to lure it out of it's cave with the bombat, the cave shark will start to move extremely slowly and try to go back to it's dome after it leaves it, it will still try to follow your bait while in the dome, albeit it with extremely slow moving and turning speed. However, it will never bite your bait, requiring a restart to fix.

# Sea Caverns
The Sea Caverns are the deep caves under the Bay and The Open Sea where many fish live, requiring higher-level rods to even reach and a Sinker rod upgrade or flick to properly navigate.

Exploration of the Caverns is impossible without the use of a Lightbulb (which is unlocked by catching an intact Bulbfish ).

Map of the caves under The bay and The Open Sea

Contents

1 Sub-regions

2 Fish

2.1 Small fish

2.2 Medium fish

2.3 Large fish

2.4 Huge fish

Sub-regions [ ]

Bay caves. Located directly below the player's starting area, the Bay . The highest region of the Caverns, and easily reached with a Sinker lure. It is necessary to move forwards and backwards to successfully pull fish out of the Bay caves, and so requires the use of a boat.

The Nightfish Sire cave. Below and slightly to the east of the Bay caves, the Nightfish Sire cave can be found. This is a single large, circular cavern usually filled with 3-4 Nightfish Sire , and occasionally Nightfish . Directly above the entrance to this cave, the Explorer hat can be found.

Central cavern. The largest internal section of the western Caverns, a semicircular chamber with a large opening in the ceiling. It is located to the east of the Bay caves and the Nightfish Sire cave, and west of the chasm. It also opens into the Cave Shark's lair. The floor of the central cavern sits at the maximum depth of the Master Rod without upgrades. Bonefish are found on the floor here.

Cave Shark's lair. A small chamber west of the central cavern, below a rocky outcrop. Home to the elusive Cave Shark .

The chasm. Sometimes called the ocean cleft or the basin (not to be confused with the coral basin). The chasm is a huge trench in the seafloor, immediately west of the reef wall. It goes down to maximum depth, although the walls continue down beyond the bounds of the map. Theoretically one of the deepest areas in the game. There is a small cave in the west wall that connects it to the central cavern.

Fish [ ]

The Caverns are home to a range of rare fish, some of which come up to the surface at night and during storms .

Small fish [ ]

Yang - In a small cave directly below the Bay, in a school of three.

Yin - In the open water of the chasm, at Cavern level, in a school of three.

Bombat - Lines the walls of the central caverns, can be lured out with bombs or rocks.

Snobfish - sometimes following nightfishes

Medium fish [ ]

Snoutfish - In the upper sections of the Caverns west of the chasm, but may also follow other fish to the surface.

Nightfish - Occasionally found in the Nightfish Sire cave, following Nightfish Sire around in groups of two or three. Also found in deep open ocean, and comes to the surface at night.

Large fish [ ]

Cavefish - Common, found in most parts of the caverns west of the reef wall. May come out of the caverns at night.

Stankfin - Usually found in the Bay caves but can be found in other sections west of the reef wall as well.

Nightfish Sire - Exclusively found in the Nightfish Sire cave.

Thresher - Commonly in the large, open central cavern.

Bonefish - Sleeps on the floor of the central cavern. Must be woken with a bomb.

Gorbon - In the caves to the west of the chasm.

Huge fish [ ]

The Sea Caverns are home to only one Huge fish - the Cave Shark . It can be found in its lair west of the central cavern, and is regarded as one of the most difficult fish to catch on Realism mode .

The Yang and Cavefish, in the Bay caves.

# Fish
Fish are the primary fauna in Cat Goes Fishing , recorded in the Catalog . Fish can be found in all regions of the world map, such as the Bay , Coral Reef , Caverns , and Silo . They can be caught and sold, or turned into bait for larger fish.

When you are reeling in Small or Medium fish, pieces of your catch can be stripped off and eaten by larger fish, leaving bare bones after three bites. Normally, bones are worthless, but if you are wearing the Bones hat , they are worth twice as much as an intact fish.

Small Fish [ ]

The smallest size category of fish is Small. Small fish can be hooked by any size of bait, and as such are the only fish that will not steal small bait off the hook. They can be blocked from eating Large bait using the Bait Guard rod upgrade. When thrown back in the water, they create medium bait.

Most Small fish will leave when night comes, and return the next morning.

# The Silo
The Distant Ocean , also known as the Silo , lies on the eastern end of the map, beyond the Coral Reef and the Drop-Off . It is a mysterious, eerie stretch of open water above the entrance to an abandoned nuclear silo, and one of the deepest areas in the game.

Around the waters of the Silo is a radar jammer which makes your sonar useless, unless you have unlocked the Tech Boat , because it penetrates radar jammers.

Sub-regions [ ]

Shallow Silo [ ]

The waters above the deep Silo, closer to the surface. The vast majority of the fish in this region can be found here.

Deep Silo [ ]

Lies at the lower limit of the map. Using a Lightbulb lure, the entrance to the nuclear silo can be seen in the darkness.

Fish [ ]

The Silo's waters contain a large number of unique fish.

Small fish [ ]

Chaw - Swims in large schools and swarms bait.

Snuggler - In the outer areas of the Silo, where Chaw spawn less frequently.

Babyjaw - In the upper waters, follows Yellowjaw in groups of two or three.

Tim - In the Deep Silo, swimming at small groups.

Medium fish [ ]

Bitterfish - Above the Silo at most depths, from the midwaters down.

Furrow - Above the Silo at most depths, from the shallower waters down.

Yellowjaw - In the upper waters above the Silo.

Slimey - In the Deep Silo, often close to the seafloor.

Lamby - In the Deep Silo, often close to the seafloor.

Goldbeard - Usually swims beyond boat range, at the very edges of the map in the shallower waters.

Ghast - In the Deep Silo, often close to the seafloor and close to the Silo cliff wall.

Sodafish - Usually swims beyond boat range, at the very edges of the map in the midwaters.

Powerfin - Usually in medium depth near the center of the Silo.

Zub - Deep down and to the left of the Silo in the Fossil Area.

Large fish [ ]

Piercer - In the shallows and mid-ocean waters.

Thistle - In the deep to mid-ocean waters.

Lampis - In the Deep Silo, slightly higher than Geiger.

Old Cusper - In the Deep Silo, near or around Germ 's den.

Geiger - In the waters directly over the submerged door to the nuclear silo itself.

Huge fish [ ]

Abyssal - Found far down in the Deep Silo, and is elusive despite its size. It requires the Mega Hook to catch.

Magnalav - A mysterious fish that rests in the distant ocean past the silo and out of reach without using the tech boat, unless one of the multiple methods of making it appear are used.

Great Cusper - A mutated Cusper that will slowly seek out Large Bait when in the water for long enough.

The Magnalav is a Huge fish with a lumpy purple body and rounded fins. It is fat like an egg. Originally added as part of the 2019 Halloween update in a buggy state, it was removed and later added back in with the August 2022 update. In the 2023 Ancient Ancestors update, its approach conditions were changed, and it became easier to attract.

Location [ ]

The Magnalav can be found anywhere in the ocean, as long as you aren't at shore it will slowly make it to your boat. It will appear on the player's Sonar if they remain still for some time. However, it will quickly disappear if the player makes any movements.

Catching Guide [ ]

The Magnalav's behavior is complex and very involved. There are 2 proven ways of catching the Magnalav as of now.

You need to idle for a while and wait for it to appear on your Sonar. This can be accomplished at any part of the map. Once it appears, you should lightly tap your movement, ensuring that it is not too much that it goes into the depths, until you arrive back at the shore. Once back at the shore, you must un-equip the boat. The Magnalav will not swim away if you throw your bait. You will need a mega hook and a large bait. You should also unequip Sinker , as the Magnalav approaches very slowly towards bait.

The second method involves changing your time, which allows catching the Magnalav on your boat. Change it to the 31st of October, 2019 at 19:15, and go to The Silo . There appears to be a glitch where setting your time to any time before the release date of Cat Goes Fishing also triggers the spawning of the Magnalav, however, this needs to be tested further and/or confirmed by the developer to be intentional. The Magnalav will shoot up from the ground and follow your boat. Repel is needed for Bloopers and Old Cuspers , however if you use a trawler the bloopers will get caught in that instead. The player cannot have the Sinker equipped. Then cast your bait down. The Magnalav will slowly come towards your bait as long as you do not flick, move, or reel in. Once it gets sufficiently close, change your time back to the present, and it will hook.

Fun fact: it takes very loooooong for him to break your line, so if you dont see how broken the line is dont worry.

# Boats
Boats are the main form of transport in Cat Goes Fishing . They are purchased from the Boat Shop, which is unlocked at Level 8. The boat you have determines the distance you can travel across the map.

When the player moves out to sea, they cannot sell fish directly. Instead, fish must be thrown backwards into the boat, where they are stored. Caught fish can be sold in bulk once the player returns to shore.

Boats do not have to be purchased in order of price or quality.

Contents

1 Basic Boat

2 Good Boat

3 Speedboat

4 Dark Boat

5 Luxury Boat

6 Collier

7 Tech Boat

Basic Boat [ ]

A slow moving boat that can't travel far from shore.

The first boat available to a new player, the basic boat cannot travel very far from shore. It can hold a maximum of 5 fish.

The Basic Boat is purchased from the Boat Shop for $35,000.

Good Boat [ ]

An improved boat that holds up to eight fish,

The Good Boat can travel further than the Basic Boat, allowing the player to reach the Open Ocean . It moves at approximately the same speed as the Basic Boat, and can hold a maximum of 8 fish.

The Good Boat is purchased from the Boat Shop for $120,000.

Speedboat [ ]

Travels faster than other boats but holds fewer fish.

The Speedboat travels further than the Good Boat, reaching approximately the midpoint of the Open Ocean. It moves faster than either the Basic Boat or the Good Boat, but can only hold a maximum of 6 fish.

The Speedboat is purchased from the Boat Shop for $240,000.

Dark Boat [ ]

Decreases battery drain on Lightbulbs by half!

The Dark Boat travels further than the Speedboat, and is able to reach almost to the reef wall. It moves at approximately the same speeds as the Basic Boat and Good Boat, but as an added bonus it causes the Lightbulb timer to count down slower, extending the duration. It can hold a maximum of 6 fish.

The Dark Boat is purchased from the Boat Shop for $360,000.

Luxury Boat [ ]

Travels quite far but isn't very fast.

Prior to the Halloween 2019 update, the Luxury Boat was the best boat in the game. It moves at approximately the same speeds as the Basic Boat and Good Boat, but travels much further than either and is able to reach the Coral Reef . It can hold a maximum of 8 fish.

The Luxury Boat is purchased from the Boat Shop for $720,000.

Collier [ ]

Travels quickly and holds many fish.

The Collier was, before the release of the Tech Boat, the best boat in the game, and was added during the Halloween 2019 update. It moves at the same speed as the Speedboat but has a vastly superior range to any previous boat, allowing the player to reach as far as The Silo . It can hold a maximum of 16 fish.

The boat's name "O.R.P KUWETA KOTA" is a reference to a polish Cat Goes Fishing youtuber Mandzio that popularized it in the polish community and is mentioned in the credits.

The Collier is purchased from the Boat Shop for $1,500,000.

Tech Boat [ ]

Penetrates sonar jammers.

The Tech Boat allows the use of sonar in The Silo area and beyond to the Plat eau and Furthest Ocean , without any interference. It holds 16 fish and travels further and faster than the Collier.

The Tech Boat can be purchased from the Boat Shop for $5,000,000 after you have caught the " Phase " introduced in the "Ancient Ancestors" update and the " Abyssal ".

The Josie is a rare and particularly special Large fish that lives in the waters of the coral basin. It is purple in colour with a distinctive heart-shaped tail and tall fin. It is very valuable and, along with the Ancient Kingfish, gives the most Fishing XP out of any fish when caught. [ ]

The Josie is one of the few ways to unlock the game's secret ending. This ending prior to the 2022 update couldn't be unlocked if the Josie is fished up and sold, since it didn't respawn and there was only ever one Josie in the game then.

Since the 2022 update, if you catch her, she will respawn and follow fish in the Silo area, like Goldbeard and Snuggler . You can still attain the secret ending this way. Its very useful to reach max level. If you will rescue Josie by grab she as cat and release she will help you.

Location [ ]

The Josie is found at the easternmost point of the Coral Reef (at the far east end of the coral basin) and sometimes to the far east of the Silo as well where it is usually found trailing a Goldbeard . It swims in the midwater region above the Depths.

A very easy way to locate it is by closing the game and re-opening it, the Josie will always spawn far east of the Coral Reef , West of the Bloopers , you must act quickly though as it will continue swimming East until it reaches past the Silo where it will move freely wherever it desires.

Catching Guide [ ]

The Josie is a relatively simple Large fish to catch, and requires large bait. It moves quite slowly and but it is easy to attract its attention. In Realism Mode , it will pull against the line violently and can easily drag your line all way down to the ocean floor, it is best to lure the Josie near the boat before reeling it up .

HOWEVER, it is in the player's best interests to not catch the Josie at all, or to wait until after they have unlocked the game's ending to do so.

If in the boat, Josie will jump out if you get close to an area where it can be sold.

Tip : You can catch Josie, release it, find it again quickly and repeat, which causes the player to get massive amounts of experience. If you can't find Josie after doing that, use a nightfall hat and use another hat. Quickly go to the easternmost point of the Coral Reef and using a deep sonar, you can spot the Josie swimming away.

Another thing, after catching and releasing a Josie once already the same fish will suddenly become incredibly simple to reel in by not fighting at all, but the effect diminishes once you exit the game.

Reaching the Secret Ending [ ]

The only two things required to unlock the secret ending are the Josie and the ability to swim. Swimming is unlocked when the player reaches level 25.

Using Q to enter the water and arrow keys to navigate, exit your boat and swim down to the Josie. Use CTRL to grab onto it, and hold on (a message reading "Don't let go!" will appear).

The Josie will proceed to swim east (or below deep cave) until it exits the map boundary, taking you with it. When you leave the screen, credits will begin to roll, and you will be awarded the Escape The Map achievement .

After this, pressing Q again or restarting the game will put the player back in their boat as if nothing had happened.

Alternative Methods [ ]

Previously it was possible to reach the secret ending by manually Swimming to the end of the map, but this was patched out with the 2022 update. However, there are still a couple of unintended methods of reaching the secret ending without using Josie:

When nighttime begins, all Seraphs will swim to the bottom edge of the map. If you grab one while swimming, it will eventually take you to the credits.

If you bait a Great Cusper old cuspers and any other large or huge (like magnalav ) fish under the map upwards with a small or medium bait, it will eat your bait and begin to swim downwards again. If you grab onto it during this time, it will also take you to the credits.

Interestingly, using any non-Josie method will give you a shorter, more cut down credits scene.

Although Sizzer are quite easy to find, they can be hard to catch. When one Sizzer is hooked, the others within view range will try to snap your line and free your catch. The Flick rod upgrade can help dislodge the attacking Sizzer as you reel in your catch.

Alternatively, it is possible to selectively drive away any other Sizzer using bombs , but this is risky as there is potential to scare off your target as well.

The Sodafish is a very rare and valuable Medium fish. It is tied for most valuable fish with the Dragon . Unlike most other fish, the Sodafish has a different sprite depending on which way it is facing - the soda attached to its body is only visible on one side.

Location [ ]

It can be found a bit beyond the Silo , but may very rarely be found near the coral reef or in other parts of the map. It usually swims at mid-ocean depths, above total darkness.

Catching Guide [ ]

The Sodafish cannot be caught using normal bait . When you approach it make sure not to get too close, or it will steal your bait and cause the cast to fail. You need to make the Sodafish flip upside down so that the can attached to it falls off. When that happens, quickly rotate around the fish and hook the can (do not leave it or else the fish will dash for the can and get it back). After you have the can, the Sodafish can be caught as normal.

The difficulty is often maneuvering the Sodafish into a good position in the first place. Since it often appears at the far end of the map, beyond boat range, your movement is limited. It may be necessary to lure the Sodafish back to an area under your boat where it is easier to flip it upside down.

You will need:

Collier boat or Tech boat (to reach the edge of the Silo)

Sinker and Glitterbait rod upgrades (Glitterbait is not essential but helps to keep the fish's attention)

Lightbulb and Bait Hat are optional but helpful

The Tech boat is advised so you can use the sonar and also maneuver the hook more effectively.

Before you go for the Sodafish itself, it can be helpful to clear the area of other fish using bombs. For this, the rod upgrades Detonator and Bomb Stack are very effective. Be careful when using bombs that you do not scare off the Sodafish itself. Or you can just swim and grab it.

# Tools
Tools are equipment that allow for ease of navigation underwater. They can be purchased from the Tool Shop, which is unlocked at Level 14. The player must have completed a certain number of quests before each item becomes available for purchase. All items in the Tool Shop are a one-time purchase.

Any of the tools can be turned on and off individually by clicking them again in the Tools Shop.

Minimap [ ]

The minimap is a small square display in the top-left of the player's screen. It shows a widened view of the water directly below the hook, allowing the player to see deeper fish. However, since it displays a true picture of the water and not a sonar display, it isn't helpful at depths or during the night if the player is not using a Lightbulb .

The minimap costs $5,000, and becomes available at Level 6 with the Tool Shop. It requires 9 completed quests to unlock.

Sonar [ ]

The sonar upgrade places a green sonar display in the top-right of the player's screen. The sonar displays fuzzy shapes corresponding to the size of the fish below the boat, and has a much wider radius than the minimap. This allows the player to more easily target specific fish or thread the lure between schools. The sonar is also visible on the boat as a black screen pointing towards the water.

The sonar costs $125,000, and becomes available at Level 6 with the Tool Shop. It requires 19 completed quests to unlock.

Deep Sonar [ ]

Deep sonar is an upgrade to the sonar. It has a much higher resolution and provides the sonar with a much clearer image that shows the outlines of each fish. It also allows the sonar to follow the player's lure down into the caverns and other deep places. It can be turned on and off independently from normal sonar.

The deep sonar costs $1,200,000, and becomes available at Level 35. It requires 29 completed quests to unlock.

It requires a Mega hook and large bait.

To catch Elder Orwellian you have to catch an Orwellian, place it on your boat, and release it over the hook by swimming, grabbing it, and releasing it in the water. The fish must be alive. the Orwellian will transform into an Elder Orwellian in a flash of lightning, bite the lure and start dragging the line directly downwards, even on classic mode. If Elder Orwellian hits a rock as its traveling downwards it will become unresponsive, but if it exits the map without hitting a rock it will stop fighting, allowing you to catch it. # Fishing Rods
The fishing rod is the core tool used by the Cat in Cat Goes Fishing . Although you start with only a basic rod, it can be upgraded as the game progresses, Successive upgrades allow you to cast farther, sink deeper, reel faster, and tackle more and more challenging fish. In addition, better rods have more slots available to install useful rod upgrades .

Although you automatically change to a new rod once it is purchased, you can go back to using one of your old rods by clicking on it in the rod menu. Upgrades are specific to an individual rod, and do not transfer over (but they are also not removed from one rod if you buy another one). A newly-purchased rod will always have no upgrades, and upgrades get progressively more expensive on higher-level rods.

Basic Rod [ ]

The Basic Rod is the player's starting rod, obtained for free at the beginning of the game. It has a short line and cannot reach the deep ocean or touch the seafloor.

It has three slots available for rod upgrades.

The Basic Rod is required for obtaining the Clever Cat achievement .

Good Rod [ ]

The Good Rod can be purchased from the Rod Shop for $750. It has a relatively short line (although longer than the Basic Rod) and cannot reach the depths without the use of a rocket .

It has four slots available for rod upgrades.

Power Rod [ ]

The Power Rod can be purchased from the Rod Shop for $10,000. It has a long line and is capable of reaching parts of the seafloor without assistance.

It has five slots available for rod upgrades.

Quick Rod [ ]

The Quick Rod can be purchased from the Rod Shop for $35,000. It has a longer line than the Power Rod and is able to touch most parts of the seafloor, but cannot descend into the Caverns . It also has the fastest unupgraded reel-in speed of any rod.

It has six slots available for rod upgrades.

Great Rod [ ]

The Great Rod can be purchased from the Rod Shop for $180,000. It has a longer line than the Quick Rod and is able to explore the seafloor and descend a decent distance into the Caverns.

It has seven slots available for rod upgrades.

Master Rod [ ]

The Master Rod can be purchased from the Rod Shop for $320,000. It is the best rod in the game, and capable of exploring the majority of the Caverns without assistance. With the addition of the Explorer hat , it can descend to the deepest sections of the game - the bottom of the Silo , the lower parts of the coral basin caves and empty caves, and the chasm.

It has eight slots available for rod upgrades.

# Equipment
In Cat Goes Fishing , the player can obtain a variety of equipment to help them navigate the game world and catch larger, more challenging fish.

Equipment is purchased from various shop tabs that unlock across the top of the screen as the player levels up, including the Rod Shop, Boat Shop, and Hatalog.

Contents

1 Fishing Rods

2 Rod Upgrades

3 Lures

4 Tools

5 Boats

6 Hats

Fishing Rods [ ]

Main article: Fishing Rods
The rod is the player's core tool, used to catch fish. The player begins with a Basic Rod but is able to upgrade through the Rod Shop, allowing them to cast farther, sink deeper, reel faster, and tackle more and more challenging fish.

Rods have upgrade slots, allowing the player to purchase and install useful rod upgrades. Higher-tier rods have more available slots.

Rod Upgrades [ ]

Main article: Rod Upgrades

Rod upgrades are available for purchase through the Rod Shop menu, by clicking on an equipped rod. They provide an array of useful bonuses that make catching fish easier, and are unlocked gradually through game progression.

Each rod comes with upgrade slots which are specific to that rod, so the player can build different upgrade sets on different rods.

Lures [ ]

Main article: Lures
Lures are purchasable, one-use items that are purchased from the Lure Shop and can be attached to your line. Each lure has a different effect, such as providing light in deep waters or allowing the player to catch Huge fish.

Tools [ ]

Main article: Tools

The minimap, sonar and deep sonar are all purchased from the Tool Shop. They allow the player to better navigate the water, first with the use of the wider-screen minimap and later with increasingly high-resolution sonar displays.

Boats [ ]

Main article: Boats
The Boat Shop, unlocked at Level 7, allows the player to purchase a boat and travel away from the shore. This opens up the ability to fish in other regions of the map, giving access to newer, more valuable fish.

The Basic Boat, which is the first boat available to the player, can only travel a short distance away from the shore. Successive boats will increase your maximum travelling range.

Hats [ ]

Main article: Hats

Hats can be fished up from the ocean, and after they are unlocked in this way they may be purchased from the Hatalog while the player is not out at sea. Hats have a variety of helpful passive effects which may aid the player in a variety of ways (such as increasing the effectiveness of different lures or making the boat move faster).

# Regions
Map of the Cat Goes Fishing world as of the most recent update. Click on the image and then "see full image size" for better quality.

The Cat Goes Fishing game world is divided into distinct regions based on depth and distance from the shore. Different regions contain different fish, with the difficulty of individual catches increasing the further the player goes from shore.

While there are no distinct borders between different zones of the map, they are usually separated by underwater land masses, such as the wall that divides the open sea from the reef.

The easternmost regions of the map (as well as some of the deeper parts of the chasm) are irradiated, which prevents sonar from functioning without the Tech boat .

Contents

1 The Bay

2 Open Sea

3 The Coral Reef

4 The Drop-Off

5 The Distant Ocean

6 The Plateau

7 The Furthest Ocean

The Bay [ ]

Main article: The Bay

The Bay is the area closest to shore, where the player begins the game. It is the westernmost section of the map, and contains mostly common, easy-to-catch starter fish such as the Cuddlefish , Mustardfish and Cowfish .

Open Sea [ ]

Main article: Open Sea

The open sea is the region directly east of the Bay. It contains a large number of rarer, more colourful fish, but also several species of fish found in the Bay such as Seraphs and Grumpers .

The Coral Reef [ ]

Main article: Coral Reef

The Coral Reef is an area filled with rare and diverse varieties of fish not seen closer to shore, such as the Hamahed , Swaller and the Yellowjack . It is separated from the open ocean by a tall undersea cliff, the reef wall. Ontop of which is a thriving coral dome filled with many unique and colourful fish like the Celad and Pink Kisser .

The Drop-Off [ ]

Main article: Drop-Off

The Drop off is an area east of the coral reef separated by a big cave known as "The empty cave". Not many fish live in the drop off. It is the main area in which the Dragon and Shaku is found.

The Distant Ocean [ ]

Main article: The Distant Ocean

The Silo, also known as the Open Ocean or Distant Ocean is a deep area of open water that lies beyond the Drop-Off. On the seafloor lies the disused entrance of a nuclear silo which also acts as a sonar jammer, and the surrounding waters are heavily irradiated to the point that certain fish are becoming mutated. Within this area many strange fish can be found, like Chaw , Lampis and the eerie Abyssal .

The Plateau [ ]

Main article: Rocky Plateau and Kelp Plateau

The Plateau is a large, flat section beyond the Silo. # Catalog
The Catalog as it appears in-game

The Catalog is a core tool that the player has access to in Cat Goes Fishing . It records fish that the player has seen and caught, providing information on the fish itself as well as listing the sell price for each fish. The Catalog sorts fish into categories based on size.

Fish that the player has seen but not caught are listed, but greyed out. A fish that was partially (or completely) eaten before it was reeled in will be shown in this state, until the player manages to catch an intact one.

The Catalog is available through a tab on the left-hand side of the screen. It is unlocked once the player reach level 3.

Upon reaching level 65 every fish (other then secret) get revealed in the catalog as hidden.

# Lures
Lures are purchasable, one-use items that can be attached to your line. The majority of lures last for one cast, and hooks and bombs are automatically removed when the line is reeled in.

Contents

1 Rocket

2 Bomb

3 Lightbulb

4 Huge Hook

5 Mega Hook

6 Trawler

Rocket [ ]

Rocket in the Lures Shop

The rocket is a one-use lure that improves your cast distance. The rocket is attached to the hook and sends it flying on cast, potentially doubling the normal casting distance of your rod. It is particularly useful in the early game, if you lack a boat, but also finds use in the late game for fishing beyond the map boundary.

The rocket costs $400, and is unlocked in the Lure Shop at level 5.

Wearing the Cowboy Hat decreases the cost of rockets to $100.

Bomb [ ]

Bomb in the Lures Shop

The bomb is a lure that explodes when fish try to take the bait, scaring away any fish in a small radius. Each bomb only lasts for one explosion, but bombs can be stacked up to five with the Bomb Stack rod upgrade. The Detonator rod upgrade allows the player to detonate the bomb on command using right-click, instead of waiting for a fish to bite. Since it is a round object, the bomb can also be used to catch Bombats (although a rock is more effective).

The bomb costs $200, and is unlocked in the Lure Shop at level 5.

Wearing the Demolition hat decreases the cost of bombs to $90, and makes the explosion radius much larger. The Surgeon hat decreases the radius and power of bomb explosions.

Keybind - B

Lightbulb [ ]

Lightbulb in the Lures Shop

The lightbulb is a lure that, when attached to your line, provides a light source for fishing at night or in the deep ocean. By default, the lightbulb lasts for four minutes. However, when wearing the Battery Pack hat, the duration is hugely extended to 40 minutes. The duration of the lightbulb is also doubled by the Dark Boat.

The extended light duration from the Battery Pack hat remains even if the player is no longer wearing that hat. Equipping the hat, buying a lightbulb, and then changing hats will allow a player to keep the longer 40-minute light duration but gain the benefit of another hat.

The lightbulb costs $1,000, and is unlocked in the Lure Shop the first time the player fishes up an intact Bulbfish . It is also the main target of Bulbeaters found in lower depths.

Keybind - L

Huge Hook [ ]

Huge hook in the Lures Shop

The huge hook is a one-use lure that allows the player to chain catches (fish that bite at a smaller fish on the hook will be hooked themselves). This can be chained indefinitely until a Large or Huge fish is caught, since nothing bites fish of that size. The huge hook also allows the player to catch most Huge fish, which otherwise will simply steal bait or pull the line without being hooked. However, it will prevent the Cave Shark from biting, and the Abyssal requires an even bigger hook.

The huge hook costs $5,000 and is unlocked in the Lure Shop the first time the Shark grabs and pulls your hook while holding a medium fish.

Keybind - H

Mega Hook [ ]

Mega Hook in the Lures Shop

The mega hook is an upgrade to the huge hook. It is a one-use lure and, like the huge hook, allows the player to chain catches. It also allows Large fish to be hooked regardless of the size of the bait. The mega hook is also the only hook that can catch the Abyssal , Shaku and Magnalav .

The mega hook costs $50,000, and is unlocked in the Lure Shop when the player reaches level 40.

Trawler [ ]

Trawler in the Lures Shop
The Trawler is an attachment for boats. When it is equipped, a white net is placed under the current boat. It can be used to catch fish which are swimming at the surface beneath the boat. This can be useful for fish that are lurking for the lure and instantly try to bite, which can be annoying for the player. The fish who are getting trapped in the net will be stuck in there and can only move a little, but are not able to reach the lure. It had a feature that if you caught enough of a certain type of fish — like mustardfish — it would make them extinct and give them an interesting new description, this feature was removed due to bugs.

It is highly recommended not to use the trawler if attempting to complete quests needing medium or small fish.

The Trawler costs $100,000, and it unlocks upon level 27.

# Hats
The Explorer hat being worn.

Hats are collectible equipment in Cat Goes Fishing that you can find floating in the water. After a hat is reeled in, it is unlocked in the Hatalog. You can choose to sell or wear a hat that has just been reeled up. They help the player fish, save money, or provide other useful bonuses when worn. A hat can be hooked and reeled in even if there is no bait on the line, using the Scouting rod upgrade .

The fishercat can only wear one hat at a time, and the player must first sell the current hat if they wish to buy a new one from the Hatalog.

The Moga is a Huge, flat, grey fish that resembles a real-world sunfish. Although it appears fairly commonly, it is rarely seen due to its aversion to light that drives it away from any lines equipped with a Lightbulb .

The Moga as seen on the radar.

The Moga swims in the Open Sea , and will follow the boat until it passes the reef wall. Using this knowledge, a player can estimate where the Moga will be at any given time.

Catching Guide [ ]

How to Catch a Moga - Cat Goes Fishing- Caverns and Coral
As with other Huge fish, a huge hook and large bait are required to catch the Moga. Since it will flee from a Lightbulb, the sonar is extremely useful in locating it.

The Moga is one of the harder fish to catch in Realism mode, thanks to its persistence, high stamina, and aversion to light. This last point requires it to be caught during the night or during a storm ; it will be extremely difficult to be reeled into the boat, as it will constantly drag the line down as soon as it leaves the depths. This means that the player will have to track their line tension through the audio cues, or use deep sonar if they have it. Moga spawns under the Tiger Shark .

You will need:

Preferably three Gauge and Improved Crank rod upgrades . (If on realism mode.)

A huge hook.

Sonar or (ideally) deep sonar, to track the Moga's movements and time your reeling with its movements.

large bait

The easiest way to catch the Moga is luring it near the surface during night or thunderstorms and then quickly reeling it into the boat. Just be aware of any large or medium fish eating your bait.

Trivia [ ]

The Moga seems to be based on the MolaMola aka Sunfish of the real world, The difference between them is that the fish in the game is afraid of light, while in real life it actually loves it, basking in it. Additionally, in the game it is quick and agile, whereas in real life it’s a very poor swimmer. So even though it’s based on this fish, it’s actually the opposite of it.

shark in catalog

Statistics [ ]

Shark

Size

Huge

Rarity

Exotic

Sell Price

$64,000

Location

The Bay

The Shark is most likely the first Huge fish a new fishercat will encounter. Like most Huge fish, it requires a huge hook to catch. The first time that a player hooks a Shark, it will drag the line down and snap it, unlocking the huge hook in the Lure Shop.

The Shark can be useful when there are many aggressive Small or Medium fish around that may steal your bait, as it will grab any live fish on your line and shred them, creating new bait equivalent to the size of the hooked fish.

Location [ ]

The Shark swims low down (but not in darkness) in the waters of the Bay .

Catching Guide [ ]

The Shark only eats live bait - specifically, Medium fish. To prepare to catch it, first get medium or large bait on your hook, and then equip the huge hook and cast your line. Catch a Medium fish, and float your line down to the area where the Shark swims. Once hooked, it is fairly simple to catch, even in Realism mode . Simply pull when it relents, and even though it will take your line further down again, it won't be as far as it was before you pulled.

Although it is easier to reach the Shark with an upgraded rod, it is possible to catch it using only the Basic Rod .

If you are having trouble getting your hook down to the Shark's level, using the Bait Guard or Crankbait rod upgrades or clearing a path with bombs may help.

Trivia [ ]

The Perpetual Motion achievement is awarded for chaining three bites from a Shark and other fish.

The Clever Cat achievement is gained by catching the Shark with a basic rod, classic is recommended.

The shark is one of the few fish that may be helpful to keep around, you can turn undesirable fish back into bait to catch a more valuable one.
The distance gained by the shark snagging the line can be used to get your line on the sea floor, which will allow to snap your line and unlock the flick rod upgrade, one of the best upgrades in the game.

The Yin is a small fish with a white body and rounded black fins. Elusive and curious, it has a tendency to follow bait , but will not bite the hook unless it is swimming alongside its partner, the Yang .

Location [ ]

The Yin is found in the open water directly west of the reef wall, far down at the point where the water becomes totally black. It is always found in a school of three, and is easy to find by simply following the reef wall down.

Catching Guide [ ]

The Yin and Yang will only bite the hook when they are next to each other. This is not a trivial task, as the Yin is found far east close to the reef wall and the Yang is found on the far left-hand side of the map below the Bay.
How To Catch Yin and Yang - Cat Goes Fishing
When approaching either the Yin or Yang with bait, they will begin to follow it. Unlike other fish, it is very difficult to break the attention of the Yin and Yang, and they will follow the lure indefinitely as long as it does not get too far away. The player must use this tendency to lead one school to the other, at which point they will begin circling around each other. When they are in this circling motion, they can be hooked.

You will need:

Crankbait and Sinker rod upgrades , for avoiding the attention of fish and leading the Yin through the water.

A Luxury Boat or better, since you need to travel between the Bay and the reef wall.

Lightbulb lure (both Yin and Yang are found in darkness).

Nightfall hat (not essential, but can make navigating easier as there are less small fish to avoid at night).

It may be convenient to clear the Yang's cave, because the larger fish will try to eat anything you catch as you are trying to get it out.

Note that the Yin and Yang both have very long respawn times. If you are trying for a specific one and fail to hook it, do not clear out an entire school, or you will have to wait for all three of them to respawn. It is more convenient to back off until both schools have returned to their respective homes, and then try the journey again. Sometimes, the fish that you did not lead to a new location will refuse to bite. In this case, try the journey from the other direction.

Tip: You can catch a hat and lure the fish into eachother, then come back later.

# Open Sea
The Coral Tree, the most prominent feature of the Open Sea

The Open Sea is an open stretch of water east of the Bay . It is largely featureless, but the seafloor features a mass of seaweed, a strange coral tree, and several openings into the Sea Caverns . The Open Sea ends at the reef wall, which separates it from the Coral Reef . It is home to a large number of diverse fish .

Sub-regions [ ]

Reef Wall [ ]

The cliff face which forms the western edge of the Coral Reef , also known as the reef wall. It acts as the eastern-most edge of the Open Sea and separates it from the Coral Reef. Some unique fish are found swimming close to it such as the Torby .

Sea Caverns [ ]

The Sea Caverns under the Open Sea, many valuable fish can be found there such as the Thresher and the Cave shark .

Coral Tree [ ]

The Underwater tree Is a tree on the floor of the Open Sea, there are many unique fish around it such as the Sassy , Skrimp and Spotter .

Chasm [ ]

The Chasm is a deep cave-like structure seemingly without a floor where all day-time only fish go to sleep during the night. The only fish close to the Chasm area is the Yin and the occasionally swimming by Gorbon

Fish [ ]

There are a large number of fish that are unique to the Open Ocean, including the Skitter, Puff, Sizzer and Bagu. Many fish found in the Bay can also be found here, including the Seraph, Sibilfin and Turgeon.

# Seer

Seer is a Hat . It displays quest fish on the sonar with a red icon.

Usefulness [ ]

When looking for an elusive fish the Seer hat can be invaluable, as it allows the player to quickly scan the map with the sonar instead of painstakingly scouring an area. It also helps when receiving a quest for a previously unknown fish, as it provides a clear pointer even if the player doesn't know what they're looking for.

However, it is one of the most expensive hats in the game (behind the Kardinal ) and so buying it on demand isn't easy until the player has a lot of spare cash available.

Fishing up the Seer hat is one way to obtain the Super Swag achievement , which is awarded for fishing up a Legendary or Mythic hat.

How to Acquire [ ]

Like most hats in Cat Goes Fishing , the Seer hat is obtained by finding it underwater and fishing it up. Hats can be hooked even if the bait has already been eaten (provided the Scouting rod upgrade is equipped).

While searching for hats, they show up as distinctive icons on the sonar and deep sonar. In addition, hats seen floating in the water will eventually sink to the seabed if not caught, requiring the use of the Sinker rod upgrade to find them and pull them up.

Catching Guide [ ]

The Shaku requires Large Bait and a Mega Hook to catch, and a Lightbulb to see in the dark. The Shaku requires the player to have unlocked Swimming in order to catch it. Cast your line, then swim down alongside the bait. When you reach the Shaku, hover your bait just above its head, then grab and hold onto its face. The Shaku will start blinking, and then will lunge to grab your bait. It can only be caught while blinking. Using this method, Radar is disabled. It can be helpful to catch the Dragon first to avoid it interfering, but this is not necessary.

Special thanks to Powr1ngHow1ng for being the first to locate, catch, and discover the method of catching the Shaku.

The Swisher is a Small, elusive fish with a pale blue body. Perhaps because it is nocturnal, it has large, staring eyes. It gathers in small schools, so although it is hard to find it is possible to make a decent profit by catching a whole school of Swisher.

The Swisher is found in the Drop-off. # Rogachev
This oddly shaped fish sucks up its friends and launches them for protection; they share a symbiotic relationship.

The Rogachev is a Medium fish with a yellow body and purple at the end of its fins. It has a distinctive long mouth.

Location [ ]

The Rogachev is normally found in the Kelp Plateau area just before deep waters, which is reachable via the Tech Boat . It is almost always found with a few Flechette at the Kelp Plateau.

Catching guide [ ]

To catch the Rogachev you need to have at least medium bait like a Snobfish or Roundfin , though in the Kelp Plateau your best choice for bait are Flechette , Lil Cheesefin or Rare Fruit , which are all found semi-deep down. It is recommended to have: Bomb Stack and Detonator on your rod when catching it. Bombs are very handy to deter nearby Bulbeaters and Flechettes . A lightbulb is also highly recommended.

Rogachev will shoot out nearby Flechette through its mouth to prevent the player from hooking it. Therefore, it is recommended to stay decently above where wild Flechette hang out above the cave system.

The Rogachev doesn’t fight back all too much in Realism Mode , but Gauge can still be useful.

The Regina is a Large red fish with a yellow underbody. It has the distinctive crown mark imprinted on her head, being a Queen as a partner of the Ancient Kingfish .
The Regina is usually found swimming alongside a Bishop and its name comes from the word Reginal , meaning queenly.

Location [ ]

The Regina is found in the Rocky Plateau area which is reachable via the Tech Boat . It resides in the first cave which must be broken by a Buffalofish . Once the cave is broken, Regina will swim out and stay around The Plat eau area. At this point, Regina may swim at any depth - from deep water all the way up your boat.

Catching guide [ ]

Catching the Regina cannot be done by normal means if the Ancient Kingfish hasn't already been caught. The only way it will take your bait is if you're in the process of reeling the Ancient Kingfish in. In that case, Regina will sacrifice herself and swim to your hook. A large bait is needed. Alternatively, after you catch the Ancient Kingfish and wait for a while, the Regina will swim back to the Ancient Kingfish cave where it can be easily caught with a large bait.

# Cat Goes Fishing
Cat Goes Fishing follows the fishing adventures of the main character, the aptly named Cat. The game does not have a set storyline or plot and is mainly upgrade based.

Although there is no true end to the game, there are certain milestones that most players will eventually reach. This includes: catching the first Huge fish , catching the most expensive fish , and completing the Catalog . There is also a secret ending followed by credits that is available after reaching level 25, using the rare Josie fish or a Seraph .

[ ]

The game's art is derived from a concept called "Fishing Girl" by game developer Daniel Cook. The game concept was similar, although the player was a bear, who ultimately tried to reach another bear at the other side of an ocean. Daniel Cook created a prototyping challenge of this in 2008, and it was turned into Flash, Unity, and Xbox Indie games before Cat Goes Fishing was released.

Cat Goes Fishing mentions Fishing Girl as the "original design" in its credits.
